Design System · v1 · Phase 1 of 3

Osbert - a system for hospitality.

Revenue recovery infrastructure for local venues. This document defines every token, component, and tone of voice that powers the product. Warm, considered, commercially sharp - built so an operator opens it on a Tuesday morning and thinks "this gets it."

03   Space, radius, light

Generous, soft, never bubbly.

A 4-pixel base with named tokens for the common moves. Radii get more generous as a surface gets more pill-like - buttons sit at 10px, badges at full pill, cards at 14px. Shadows are tinted warm - never cold grey - so depth still feels like the room.

04   Iconography

One line weight, one library.

Every icon in Osbert comes from Lucide (lucide.dev) - a single stroke-drawn set rendered at a 2px line weight in currentColor. Icons mark actions, states and places - never decoration. In the app they render exclusively through the shared <ob-icon> component, which owns the registry: if an icon isn't registered there, it isn't in the product.

Quiet inputs that get out of the way.

07   Status badges

The colour-coded language of the queue.

Operators scan these constantly. Each status earns a colour for a reason - amber moves toward action, green walks the journey from approval to return, red flags the careful work, neutral steps back. The strongest treatment is reserved for the moment a customer comes back.

15   Voice

Three voices, one venue.

The product writes on behalf of the venue - so tone is a feature, not a style guide. UI speaks like a capable colleague. Standard outreach is warm and unhurried. Grievance recovery is careful, human, and never automated-feeling.

i

Product UI

How the interface speaks to the operator. Smart, warm, direct. Like a colleague who knows the industry.

EMPTY STATE
No opportunities right now - check back after your next busy period.
AFTER APPROVAL
Approved. Ready to send when you are.
REVENUE EVENT
Sarah M came back. $70 recovered.
BLOCKING DECISION
We need a couple of answers before we can start. No rush - just flag these with your team.
w

Standard outreach

Default tone for re-engagement. Warm, genuine, no pressure. The venue speaks for itself.

LAPSED REGULAR
Hi Sarah, it's been a little while since we've seen you. We'd love to welcome you back - this week is a great time to book in.
FIRST-VISIT FOLLOW-UP
James, your first visit meant a lot to us. We hope to see you again soon.
r

Grievance recovery

Carefully different. Human, apologetic where appropriate. Never salesy. Must feel like a real person wrote it.

RESPONSE TO A COMPLAINT
Hi Emma, I wanted to reach out personally about your recent experience with us. What you described isn't the standard we hold ourselves to, and I'm sorry.
MAKING IT RIGHT
Tom, I heard about the issue with your booking last week. I'd love the chance to make it right - please let me know if you're open to coming back as our guest.
Tone rules - at a glance
Always
  • First-name address
  • One clear ask per message
  • Specific, not generic ("Friday at 7" not "soon")
  • The venue's actual signature
Never
  • Exclamation marks (except in success toasts)
  • Marketing words: "exclusive", "limited", "miss out"
  • Emoji in any outbound message
  • "Dear" or "valued customer"
Sometimes
  • A specific menu reference (if the operator opted in)
  • "Your usual" - if the customer has one
  • A held table offer - for high-value regulars
  • An apology - only in grievance flows
